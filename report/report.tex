\section{Introduction}
A stimulus, to visual cortex, is not a property of any single neuron. It is a pattern: thousands of cells firing together, occupying a particular region of activity space, and the geometry of that region (where it sits, how it drifts, how far it stands from other stimuli) is what carries class identity~\cite{dayan2001}. Two of the tools developed in the course probe this geometry from opposite ends. PCA goes looking for the axes that explain the most variance, ignoring any labels we might have. A supervised decoder does the opposite: it goes straight for the directions that tell the classes apart. When the two methods disagree, that disagreement is itself the result: it tells us that the class signal lives in low-variance directions PCA was never asked to find.

The MICrONS Functional Connectomics dataset~\cite{microns2025} makes this kind of comparison practical at scale. A single recording session captures roughly 8{,}000 neurons simultaneously across primary visual cortex (V1) and three higher visual areas (AL, LM, RL), exposed to three stimulus classes that span the natural--to--synthetic range. V1 is where cortical vision starts; AL, LM and RL are its dorsal-stream targets. Comparing the four areas asks a simple question: does class structure sharpen as we move up the hierarchy, or is it already there at the entry point?

% =============================================================
\section{Research Question}
Do Clip (natural movies), Monet2 (textured noise) and Trippy (synthetic waves) occupy distinguishable population states, and is that separation equally strong across V1, AL, LM and RL? A first round of results forced a second, sharper question. Is what we are seeing a real three-way structure, or just a one-dimensional naturalness boundary in disguise? And if it is a boundary, does any one area specialise on it on a per-neuron basis?

\section{Methodology}

$\quad$ \ \textbf{Session selection.} The fourteen functional sessions in MICrONS were screened on five summary statistics (neuron count, median per-neuron activity, mean pupil diameter, mean treadmill speed, and neuron distribution across brain areas). Anything outside $|z|>2$ was flagged as outlier (Fig.~\ref{fig:cohort}). Session 7\_5 sat at the median across all metrics: 8{,}194 neurons against a cohort median of 8{,}176, no behavioural red flags, and a representative profile across all the sessions.

\textbf{Preprocessing.} Eleven trials with running speed above $|v|>1$ were dropped, 2.4\% of the session, but enough to influence the response. Treadmill correlation we found during EDA. Each neuron's full timeseries was then linearly detrended (removing the $\sim$45\% photobleaching drift identified earlier) and z-scored. 453 trials remained: 377 Clip, 38 Monet2, 38 Trippy. The trial-averaged population response after this pipeline already separates the three stimulus classes by mean activity in every cortical area (Fig.~\ref{fig:psth}).

\textbf{Three experiments.} Experiment 1 collapses each trial to a single mean activity vector, fits PCA per area, and quantifies separability in two ways: balanced silhouette in the top three PCs (label-agnostic), and 5-fold cross-validated multinomial logistic regression on the full neuron matrix (supervised). Experiment 2 keeps the time axis. For each area and class it builds a trial-averaged 75 $\times$ $N$ trajectory and measures pairwise Euclidean distance frame by frame, in two spaces: the top-3 PC subspace (the picture you see in figures) and the full neuron space (the test we trust). Significance is assessed by 100 label-permutation shuffles, variability by 1{,}000 class-stratified bootstrap resamples, with the PCA basis held fixed throughout, so what the envelopes and nulls capture is uncertainty in the trajectories themselves, not in the projection. Experiment 3 runs the same pipeline of Experiment 2 inside the Clip categpry, using the three content sub-categories MICrONS provides (Cinematic, sports1m, Rendered).

\textbf{Checks.} Two confounds were obvious. Clip has roughly ten times the trials of both synthetic class, so we sub-sampled it down to 38 and recomputed. V1 has more than thirteen times AL's neuron count, so every area was sub-sampled to AL's 414 neurons (20 random draws) and the entire pipeline was rerun.

\section{Results}

\noindent\textbf{4.1 A signal exists but PCA is not aligned with it (Exp.~1).} Each trial reduces to a single $N$-dimensional vector (where $N$ is the number of neurons): component $i$ holds neuron $i$'s mean response averaged over the trial's time window. Each trial becomes a point, with one coordinate per neuron, so the whole session is a cloud of these points. The silhouette score and the classifier both work on this exact same cloud of points. Cross-validated logistic regression on it reaches 98.9\% accuracy in V1, 98.0\% in LM, 97.8\% in RL and 94.7\% in AL, comfortably above the 83.2\% majority baseline. Viewed through the top three principal components of that same cloud the picture changes: balanced silhouette oscillates around zero in every area (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp1-bars}). The class signal is there, it is indeed decodable, but it does not live in the directions PCA prioritises. A standard PC-space scatter would have shown nothing (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp1-grid}), which is what pushed us toward the time-resolved trajectory analysis that follows.

\noindent\textbf{4.2 Trajectories: an artefact, then the real answer (Exp.~2).} Now we retain the time axis. For each area and class, same-class trials are averaged frame by frame to give a trajectory: 75 successive points in neuron space, one per frame, each an $N$-dimensional vector holding the across-trial mean activity at that timepoint. Monet2 and Trippy trials had originally 113 frames but we truncated them to Clip's 75 so the three trajectories are directly comparable. We obtain three trajectories. Plotted in the PC1-PC2 plane, every area produces three clean, stationary and non-overlapping clusters (Fig.~\ref{fig:pc-clusters}). The 75 frames cluster in one spot instead of moving along a path, so each class sits at one fixed location in state space. A first significance test, run in that same top-3 PC space, returned every pair separated at $p \approx 0.01$. But that test is inconclusive. The PCA basis was fit on real-label trial averages, and the shuffled trajectories were then projected through the same basis, so the projection itself carries label information and the shuffle null can never reach what the real data reach. Therefore we re-run the test in the full neuron space, where there are no projection, and the results are completely different (Fig.~\ref{fig:honest-biased}). Table~\ref{tab:honest} reports the honest-test $p$-values. Monet2$\leftrightarrow$Trippy does not separate anywhere. The only synthetic pair that survives is Clip$\leftrightarrow$Trippy in RL ($p=0.02$). The Clip-vs-synthetic distinction, on the other hand, is consistent across every area. Both controls behave as expected: Clip sub-sampled to 38 trials reproduces the same curves, and matching every area to AL's neuron count collapses V1's apparent advantage, leaving the four areas in a tight per-neuron band (Fig.~\ref{fig:controls}).

\noindent\textbf{4.3 Inside Clip AL is a naturalness detector (Exp.~3).} By applying the same trajectory pipeline to Clip's three content sub-categories, every pair separates at $p \leq 0.02$ in every area, becoming significant by frame 2 ($\sim$267~ms post stimulus onset, Fig.~\ref{fig:exp3-three-pairs}). Moreover, after population matching, AL outperforms every other area on the two pairs that cross the natural/synthetic boundary (Cinematic$\leftrightarrow$Rendered and sports1m$\leftrightarrow$Rendered), but matches the others on Cinematic$\leftrightarrow$sports1m, which is natural-vs-natural (Fig.~\ref{fig:al-naturalness}). The simplest interpretation is that AL is not encoding content category at all. It is encoding the same naturalness axis Experiment~2 showed, now recurring within Clip itself.

\section{Discussion}
To address the first research question, the Clip-vs-synthetic boundary is robust to every control we ran and shows up in every area, so cortex does carry that signal linearly and uniformly across the visual hierarchy. The more subtle Monet2-vs-Trippy distinction is different: this boundary invisible to linear methods at the population scale. PCA can be made to display it, but only because the basis itself was informed by the labels. Both experiments end up pointing at the same organising dimension. Naturalness explains the inter-class structure in Experiment~2 and reappears as the axis along which AL gains a per-neuron edge in Experiment~3, which together suggest functional differentiation along a natural-vs-artificial axis rather than along content category.

Fitting PCA on real labels and then applying that same projection to shuffled data is a self-confirming test: as soon as the projection step is informed by the labels, the null distribution is compromised and significance has to be assessed in the unprojected space. We learned this during Experiment~2, which is why the same protection was built into Experiment~3 from the beginning.

Four limitations qualify the biological reading. 
  \emph{(i)} We cannot cleanly separate category encoding from low-level image statistics (brightness, contrast, motion energy) without stimuli explicitly matched on those statistics, which the dataset does not provide. 
  \emph{(ii)} Everything reported here comes from a single session; cross-mouse generalisation is untested. 
  \emph{(iii)} Linear methods may simply be missing non-linear structure: the Monet2$\leftrightarrow$Trippy collapse could be a limitation of the PCA model rather than of the code. 
  \emph{(iv)} We did not use the connectomics half of MICrONS, so the structural-to-functional link is still open.

Three things would push this project forward. First, a non-linear method such as CEBRA~\cite{cebra2023}, aimed directly at the Monet2$\leftrightarrow$Trippy categories, to see whether this method finds what PCA misses. Second, cross-session replication of AL's apparent naturalness-detector role, to check it is not a peculiarity of session 7\_5. Third, a test of whether AL's per-neuron advantage aligns with the wiring patterns exposed by the EM volume, which is the question MICrONS was built to make answerable.
